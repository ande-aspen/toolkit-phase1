## Resumen
Audretsch et al. provide theoretical grounding for ecosystem impact measurement across three dimensions (economic, technological, societal), drawing from the ancient Greek concept of "oikos." The paper introduces the knowledge filter concept (gap between knowledge creation and exploitation), identifies the fundamental boundary problem in ecosystem research, and synthesizes empirical findings from six studies on university-industry interaction, research network diversity, academic spinoffs, patent-based funds, and social boundaries. It frames the challenge that standard input-output production functions are inadequate for ecosystem analysis because actors serve simultaneously as both inputs and outputs.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Three dimensions of ecosystem impact
> Entrepreneurial ecosystems generate impacts across three interconnected dimensions: (1) Economic -- locational capital wealth, regional agglomeration, spillover effects, reputation attracting capital. (2) Technological -- regional innovation mechanisms, technology transfer efficiency, disruptive potential. (3) Societal -- networks among actors, co-existence of diverse organization types, governance enabling knowledge flows, social clusters along venture type, gender, and ethnicity.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15A
**Relevancia:** Complements ANDE's Condition-Result pattern. Condition domains (P, S, H, I) primarily affect technological and societal dimensions; Result domains (F, C, M) primarily reflect economic and societal outcomes. Justifies multi-domain assessment.

### INS-2: The boundary problem in ecosystem research
> Exact definitions of what ecosystems comprise are scarce and inconsistent. Ecosystems can be described by economic activities, by actors, or by boundaries. Despite 2,500+ studies by 2017, there exists no unified theory. The ecosystem metaphor can be pushed too far. For diagnostics, geographic scope must be explicitly defined and assessment must acknowledge that boundaries are porous.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15B
**Relevancia:** Important methodological caveat for ANDE Phase 1: the city-level assessment is a pragmatic choice, but ecosystems extend beyond city limits and interact with regional/national systems. Should be acknowledged in Section 2 of reports.

### INS-3: Empirical evidence on university-industry interaction and research networks
> Key findings: (1) New technology-based firms engaging in knowledge interactions with public research institutions are more likely to introduce new products. (2) Research networks with higher organizational diversity have more commercial potential, but wider geographic dispersion correlates with less innovation potential -- local density matters more than international connections. (3) University spinoffs internationalize more than non-academic counterparts.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15C
**Relevancia:** I+D domain scores should consider quality of university-industry interaction, not just patents/publications. H1 (universities with entrepreneurship) should weight commercialization capacity, not just program existence. Local research density matters more than international connections for innovation output.

### INS-4: The "oikos" framework and ecosystem self-sufficiency
> The ecosystem concept traces to Hesiod's oikos (700 BC): a self-sufficient economic unit encompassing different people, materials, and activities under governance. Translation for diagnostics: boundary definition = city scope, resource allocation = domain balance, self-sufficiency = maturity level, governance = policy + backbone organizations, entry/exit barriers = regulatory environment, spillovers = cross-domain effects.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15D
**Relevancia:** Helps explain why ecosystem maturity levels (Naciente through Autosostenible) correspond to increasing degrees of self-sufficiency and internal resource generation. Useful for theoretical framing in Section 2.

### INS-5: The knowledge filter -- gap between knowledge creation and exploitation
> The knowledge filter refers to the gap between knowledge created at universities/research labs and knowledge exploited by firms in markets. Ecosystems differ in conversion efficiency. Standard economic theory fails because profit-maximizing assumptions do not hold for public institutions (universities, government agencies) that are key ecosystem actors. Ecosystem governance requires intentional coordination mechanisms.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15F
**Relevancia:** Explains why I+D domain scores may be high (knowledge creation) while Markets scores are low (knowledge exploitation). Recommendations should address transfer mechanisms, not just increase inputs. The governance question is especially critical in developing economies where public institutions play outsized roles.

### INS-6: Ecosystem impact cannot be captured by a single aggregate measure
> Multiple inputs create multiple outputs, and the same actor can be both input and output simultaneously (e.g., a successful startup is a product of the ecosystem and a contributor as employer, role model, and potential investor). Standard production functions fail because input-output relations are multiple, inputs/outputs cannot be clearly defined.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15G
**Relevancia:** Justifies ANDE's multi-domain approach and explains why global scores should be interpreted as approximations rather than precise measurements. Ecosystem performance requires examining each domain separately.

### INS-7: Social boundaries affect knowledge flows
> Ecosystems consist of different social clusters forming boundaries along venture type (high-growth vs. lifestyle), support institution type (university vs. government), gender, and ethnicity. These social boundaries affect knowledge flows and opportunity access.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 15C (Neumeyer & Santos 2018)
**Relevancia:** Culture domain (C5 social trust) and ecosystem analysis should account for fragmentation along social lines. A nominally connected ecosystem may have invisible barriers segmenting actors.
