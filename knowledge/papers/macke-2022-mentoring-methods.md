## Resumen
Concise practice brief (2 pp.) providing a structured framework for mentoring programs within entrepreneurial ecosystems. Despite its brevity, it contains a taxonomy of 9 mentoring delivery methods, a 4-group targeting framework by entrepreneur development stage, 5 implementation principles, a quality assessment framework for scoring S4, and maturity-aligned recommendations. Positions mentoring as one component within an integrated business services ecosystem rather than a standalone program.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Nine mentoring delivery methods taxonomy
> Spectrum from low-intensity/high-scalability to high-intensity/low-scalability: traditional 1-on-1 mentors (volunteer, low cost), peer groups (facilitated), B2B events, workshops/trainings, growth counseling (focused on venture scaling), advisory boards (targeted expertise), voluntary boards of directors, entrepreneur coaching (1-on-1, compensated), and business counseling (basic plan/feasibility, often with online tools). An ecosystem relying on a single method is less mature than one deploying multiple complementary approaches.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 24A (p. 1)
**Relevancia:** When scoring S4 (Mentores), assess delivery method diversity, not just mentor count. 1-2 methods = basic; 3-5 = developing; 6+ = mature. This taxonomy structures what to look for beyond simple headcounts.

### INS-2: Four target groups for mentoring effectiveness
> Group 1 -- Aspiring/Startups: limited experience, mentors listen and help discover success requirements. Group 2 -- Challenged/Struggling: something limiting success, mentors help discover what holds them back. Group 3 -- Transitions: venture successions (seller and buyer sides), mentors optimize outcomes. Group 4 -- Growth-Oriented: seeking new markets/products/locations, must build teams and delegate, mentors provide growth planning support. Best mentors listen and ask questions rather than give direct answers, helping entrepreneurs discover their own solutions.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 24B (pp. 1-2)
**Relevancia:** Quality criteria for S4 scoring. Ecosystems serving only aspiring entrepreneurs (group 1) are less mature than those covering all four groups. Connect to GOE framework (Ref 23) for group 4. Growth mentoring addresses the critical founder-to-professional-management transition.

### INS-3: Five implementation principles for mentoring programs
> (1) Start small, learn and grow -- avoid over-engineering at launch. (2) Target by sector and/or development stage -- avoid generic open-to-all models. (3) Train, support, and manage volunteer mentors -- raw experience alone is insufficient. (4) Test mentors and curate a high-performing team -- not every experienced person is a good mentor. (5) Use mentoring only when the entrepreneur is open to it -- readiness is a prerequisite.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 24C (p. 2)
**Relevancia:** Quality checklist for S4 beyond mentor count. An ecosystem with 50 untrained volunteers may score lower than one with 15 curated, trained mentors in a structured program. The readiness principle connects to C5 (Confianza social) -- low-trust cultures may need to build help-seeking acceptance before formal mentoring programs succeed.

### INS-4: Mentoring as integrated business services component
> Mentoring should not exist in isolation but as part of a broader business services ecosystem. The paper distinguishes free services (volunteer mentoring, peer groups, community events) from compensated services (professional coaching, consulting, advisory board facilitation). Both are needed for a healthy ecosystem. Ecosystems relying entirely on volunteer/free services may lack sustainability.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 24D (p. 2)
**Relevancia:** When analyzing the S domain holistically, evaluate whether mentoring (S4) is integrated with incubation (S2), acceleration (S1), and physical spaces (S3). Fragmented services score lower. The free vs. compensated distinction is important for sustainability assessment.

### INS-5: Composite quality framework for scoring S4
> Seven assessment dimensions: (1) Existence -- any structured programs? (2) Delivery diversity -- how many of the 9 methods? (3) Target coverage -- which of the 4 entrepreneur groups served? (4) Mentor preparation -- trained and managed? (5) Integration -- connected to other support services? (6) Sustainability -- funding model (pure volunteer = fragile; mixed = developing; sustainable = mature). (7) Scale -- active mentors relative to population. Scoring guidance: 0-15 = no structured mentoring; 16-30 = 1-2 informal programs; 31-50 = formal with some training; 51-70 = multiple programs, trained/curated, integrated; 71-100 = comprehensive ecosystem.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 24E (derived from all sections)
**Relevancia:** Supplement to rubrica-scoring.md benchmarks for S4. Provides qualitative criteria that quantity metrics miss. When reviewing user-submitted S4 data, probe for quality indicators beyond mentor count.

### INS-6: Mentoring recommendations by ecosystem maturity
> Naciente (0-25): start small with volunteer traditional mentoring for aspiring entrepreneurs, 1-2 peer groups, focus on cultural acceptability of asking for help. Emergente (26-45): formalize program, train mentors, add growth counseling for GOEs, sector-specific matching, integrate with incubators. En Desarrollo (46-65): add advisory boards and coaching, 5+ delivery methods, mentor management systems, serve all 4 groups, develop compensated alongside volunteer services. Autosostenible (66-100): professional standards, mentor certification, international networks, data-driven matching, impact measurement, sustainable funding.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 24F
**Relevancia:** Use in Section 7 recommendations to ensure mentoring prescriptions match ecosystem maturity per Table 4.3. The cultural readiness insight is especially important for LatAm ecosystems where cultural norms may inhibit help-seeking behavior.
