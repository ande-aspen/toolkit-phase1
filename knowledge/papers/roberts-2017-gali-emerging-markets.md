## Resumen
The GALI report analyzes data from 2,455 ventures across 43 acceleration programs, comparing outcomes in emerging markets (EM) vs. high-income countries (HIC). Acceleration produces statistically significant gains in revenue, employees, equity, and debt in both contexts. The central finding is a structural equity gap: EM ventures have MORE revenue and MORE employees than HIC peers, but HALF the equity investment. This gap persists after controlling for sector and intellectual property, suggesting systemic barriers rather than talent deficits. The report also documents that mentor recruitment is the biggest challenge for EM programs and that network expansion post-program transfers poorly to emerging markets.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Acceleration produces positive results in emerging markets
> Across 2,455 ventures in 43 programs, accelerated ventures outperformed rejected applicants: revenue +$20,008, employees +0.68, equity +$14,333, debt +$14,096 (all statistically significant). In EM specifically: revenue +$15,090, employee growth +0.96, equity +$14,045, debt +$13,050. Equity acceleration is virtually identical across EM and HIC (~$14K), demonstrating that accelerators create temporal windows where investment flows better regardless of context.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19A
**Relevancia:** Benchmark for scoring S1 (Accelerators). If an ecosystem has accelerators but no evidence of increased equity or revenue for cohort participants, the score should reflect low effectiveness, not just presence. Functional accelerators should produce measurable investment acceleration.

### INS-2: EM entrepreneurs have strong credentials but face perception deficit
> EM entrepreneurs report MORE university education (1.65 vs. 1.45 programs), MORE prior founding experience (2.83 vs. 2.29 companies), and similar personal investment ($51,883 vs. $61,313). They select higher profit margin categories more frequently. However, half of EM investors cite lack of entrepreneurial experience as a concern vs. zero HIC investors. Zero EM entrepreneurs mention acquisition as a 5-year aspiration vs. 5 of 27 in HIC -- lower exit orientation reduces attractiveness for VCs.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19B
**Relevancia:** For F3 (Local VCs) and F4 (Active angels). The investment deficit is not explained by lack of talent but by ecosystem barriers. When an ecosystem shows high human capital but low finance, the Condition>Result pattern suggests a "connection problem," not a capacity problem.

### INS-3: The structural equity gap -- 2x disparity
> Pre-application data shows the systemic gap: EM ventures have accumulated $42,274 equity vs. $87,923 for HIC (2.1x ratio), $28,123 debt vs. $55,381 (2.0x), BUT $66,408 revenue vs. $57,203 (EM leads), and 5.04 FT employees vs. 1.40 (EM leads 3.6x). The pattern persists after controlling for sector and IP. Comparative VC volumes (2016): US $69B, India $5.6B, UK $4.8B, Mexico $526M, Kenya $49M.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19C
**Relevancia:** Direct benchmark for F1 (Total investment). For Latin American cities, Mexico $526M total country gives a ceiling context. If a local ecosystem reports $3M over 3 years, that may be reasonable given the macro context. The 2x equity gap is structural, not performance-based.

### INS-4: Sector distribution and IP -- the gap is structural, not sectoral
> EM ventures are over-represented in agriculture, artisanal, energy, financial services; under-represented in health. Average patents: HIC 0.47 vs. EM 0.23. Ventures with patents attract more equity. However, controlling statistically for sector and patents, the equity gap does NOT disappear -- it is structural. EM ventures apply later (2.8 vs. 2.1 years old) and arrive more commercially established but with less external investment.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19D
**Relevancia:** For I1 (Patents). In EM ecosystems, low patent counts are common and should not be penalized excessively against Silicon Valley benchmarks. The focus should be on whether existing patents translate into capital attraction.

### INS-5: Capital doesn't flow, but commerce does
> Rejected ventures (no acceleration) grow revenue in both contexts -- even faster in EM (+$11,043 vs. +$10,530) and grow employees much faster (+1.22 vs. +0.30). But in equity, rejected HIC ventures capture more ($8,878 vs. $8,195), and in debt the gap is dramatic ($7,048 vs. $1,566). It is equally possible to establish and operate businesses in emerging markets, but harder to attract investment to scale their initial promise.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19E
**Relevancia:** Central data point for the Condition-Result pattern. If an ecosystem shows active startups with revenue but no investment rounds, the problem is financial infrastructure, not culture or talent. High M1 with low F1-F4 = classic "connection problem."

### INS-6: Network expansion -- the hidden deficit in emerging markets
> Only 2 of 22 EM entrepreneurs reported that program connections expanded their networks (vs. 10 of 27 in HIC). Six of 13 EM program managers report mentors and experts as the hardest stakeholders to recruit. EM programs cost more ($281K vs. $125K) but produce similar-sized networks (57 vs. 55 mentors) with lower relational quality. Investment guaranteed by programs is similar (~$90K), indicating the problem is not program capital but the post-program ecosystem.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19F
**Relevancia:** For S4 (Mentors). Mere existence of mentors is insufficient; evaluate whether mentoring networks translate into sustainable post-program connections. An ecosystem with 50 listed mentors but no evidence of deals or referrals facilitated should score low.

### INS-7: Program design principles for emerging markets
> Key insights: (1) Specialize by objective -- business skills, unit economics, or investment readiness; don't mix everything. (2) Align accelerator with a specific fund's investment thesis for clear path to investment. (3) Build a continuum of programs, not a single one -- an entrepreneur may need inspiration first, then investment readiness. (4) Cultural bias exists: foreign investors find it easier to invest in expatriate founders due to cultural familiarity. (5) Context: in the US, 78% of VC goes to just three states; similar geographic concentration exists in the Global South.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19G
**Relevancia:** For Section 7 recommendations when strengthening accelerators. These principles guide HOW: specialization, fund alignment, program sequencing, and attention to cultural bias in investment. VC geographic concentration within countries mirrors ecosystem concentration within countries.

### INS-8: Top performers -- acceleration is not necessary for exceptional growth
> Top 25 EM ventures by revenue averaged $822,990 (vs. $592K in HIC), 23.9 employees (vs. 18.0). Only 33% of top revenue growers in EM participated in a program. But for equity (45%) and debt (57%), program participation correlates more with stellar results. Talent exists independently; programs help channel capital, not create entrepreneurs.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 19H
**Relevancia:** When evaluating M1 (Active startups), do not condition the score on accelerator presence. The best entrepreneurs grow with or without programs. But for F1-F2, accelerator presence does correlate with better investment outcomes. Programs are finance-enablers, not entrepreneurship-creators.
