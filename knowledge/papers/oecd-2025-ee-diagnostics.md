## Resumen
Pilot edition covering 38 OECD economies using the methodology from the companion paper (Crotti et al. 2025). Presents country scores across 10 elements, classifies ecosystems into four types (underdeveloped to well-advanced), maps policy instruments to elements, and includes three detailed case studies of successful ecosystem policies: French Tech (France), Italian Startup Act (Italy), and TechLeap (Netherlands). Provides the most comprehensive OECD-level benchmarking of LatAm ecosystems.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: LatAm country scores reveal critical structural gaps
> Mexico, Colombia, and Costa Rica classified as "mostly underdeveloped" ecosystems. Mexico scores: Institutions 4.6, Talent 3.7, Infrastructure 6.9 — among the three lowest in the entire OECD. Chile performs better but still weak on Infrastructure (17.3) and Knowledge (18.1). Mexico's Leadership score (50.1) is a notable outlier above OECD median, driven by serial entrepreneur count. Finance data unavailable for all four LatAm countries.

**Ubicación:** Section 3A (Table 3.1)
**Relevancia:** Definitive OECD benchmarks for LatAm ecosystem diagnostics. The contrast between Mexico's near-bottom Institutions/Talent/Infrastructure and above-median Leadership reveals a deeply unbalanced ecosystem. Essential reference for any Mexican city-level diagnostic.

### INS-2: Entrepreneurship output data — quantity vs. quality paradox in LatAm
> Colombia has the highest business birth rate in the entire OECD (21.7%) but near-zero equity-based startups or unicorns. Mexico and Chile have the highest job-creation expectations among OECD entrepreneurs (30.7% and 32.9%, outperforming US at 28.8%). Colombia has the most inclusive entrepreneurship (3.7% missing entrepreneurs rate). Mexico has highly dispersed startup activity geographically (HHI of 9.2 vs. 83.5 for Estonia). All four LatAm countries show 0.0 equity-based young firms per million population.

**Ubicación:** Section 3B (Annex B raw values)
**Relevancia:** Quantifies the LatAm paradox: high entrepreneurial activity and ambition coexists with near-zero productive/equity-backed entrepreneurship. Job-creation expectations without financing infrastructure = aspirational gap. Mexico's geographic dispersion is a structural strength.

### INS-3: Four ecosystem types and common weak links
> Ecosystems classified as: (1) mostly underdeveloped (Colombia, Costa Rica, Mexico); (2) started to develop (Czechia, Slovenia); (3) developed unevenly (France, Germany); (4) well advanced (US, UK, Switzerland). Leadership is the most differentiating factor between types. Common weak links across all OECD: Leadership, Markets, Knowledge, Intermediate Services. No country achieves top scores in all elements. Rapid improvements documented in France (institutions via tax reform), several countries in Markets (trade facilitation), and Portugal/Lithuania/Poland in Talent (digital skills).

**Ubicación:** Section 3C
**Relevancia:** The four-type classification provides context for positioning any ecosystem within OECD landscape. The fact that no country excels everywhere normalizes finding weaknesses in diagnostics. Rapid improvement examples show change is possible with targeted policy.

### INS-4: Policy-element mapping for startups and scaleups
> Detailed mapping of policy instruments to each of the 10 ecosystem elements. Finance addressed through grants, loans, public VC, co-investment, mezzanine, angel networks, guarantees, investment-readiness training, fintech facilitation. Talent through training programs and startup visas. Leadership through private sector consultation and alumni networks. Culture through awareness campaigns, competitions, and entrepreneurship education.

**Ubicación:** Section 3D (Table 5.1)
**Relevancia:** Direct reference for Section 7 (recommendations) of ANDE reports. Maps which policy types correspond to which ecosystem bottlenecks, enabling evidence-based policy recommendations.

### INS-5: French Tech — bottom-up ecosystem coordination model
> French Tech unifies stakeholders under one brand with a 30-person team and EUR 25M budget. Key features: 116 labeled "Capitals and Communities" with ~1,400 volunteers (70% entrepreneurs) and 6,000 startups; "I Choose French Tech" initiative for public procurement; French Tech Rise for regional VC-startup matching; 67 international communities across 52 countries. Results: incubators and serial entrepreneurs doubled, VC tripled since 2013. Success built on bottom-up mobilization of local actors as creators (not beneficiaries) of initiatives.

**Ubicación:** Section 3E
**Relevancia:** Replicable model for LatAm ecosystems. The labelling process itself forces ecosystem mapping and stakeholder alignment — a lightweight intervention with structural effects. The bottom-up principle directly addresses the entrepreneur-led development evidence from Hume (2021) and Wurth et al. (2023).

### INS-6: Italian Startup Act — power of clear legal definitions
> Italy's ISA formally defines "innovative startup" with verifiable criteria (unlisted, <5 years, <EUR 5M turnover, no profit distribution, meeting innovation criteria). Companies self-certify at Chambers of Commerce. Benefits span institutions (tax exemptions), markets (30% discount on internationalization), finance (tax deductions for equity investors, crowdfunding regulation, SME Guarantee Fund access), talent (flexible contracts, stock options), and intermediate services (certified incubator definition). VC invested increased 5-fold; incubators doubled.

**Ubicación:** Section 3F
**Relevancia:** Demonstrates how a single clear legal definition can anchor an entire ecosystem support system. The public registry creates transparency. The "innovative SME" continuation prevents premature support withdrawal. Highly relevant for P-domain recommendations in LatAm where startup definitions are often absent or vague.

### INS-7: TechLeap — curated matching over direct funding
> Netherlands' TechLeap operates as independent non-profit at arm's length from government. Core innovation: "Connector for Ventures" matches vetted companies with curated investors, rather than providing direct funding. "Incubators United" enables cross-regional collaboration sharing mentors and best practices. "Science to Impact" bridges academia-entrepreneurship gaps. "Finder" online platform maps the entire ecosystem. Netherlands performs above OECD average on 9 of 10 elements.

**Ubicación:** Section 3G
**Relevancia:** The curated matching model is replicable at lower budgets than direct funding programs. The Incubators United model addresses the fragmentation typical of nascent ecosystems. Science to Impact shows how to operationalize university-industry linkages.

### INS-8: Six common characteristics of successful ecosystem policies
> All three case studies share: (1) holistic — strengthen multiple elements simultaneously; (2) arms-length from government; (3) entrepreneur-empowering — entrepreneurs drive initiatives; (4) regional — specific actions for sub-national ecosystems; (5) ecosystem-aligned — address specific bottlenecks identified through diagnostics; (6) evolved over time based on feedback and evidence.

**Ubicación:** Section 3H
**Relevancia:** Checklist for evaluating and designing ecosystem policy recommendations. Any recommended intervention should be assessed against these six characteristics. The emphasis on arms-length independence and entrepreneur empowerment aligns with the broader evidence base on effective ecosystem building.
