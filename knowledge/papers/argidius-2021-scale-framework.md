## Resumen
Evidence synthesis from 8 years of Argidius Foundation research (2013-2021), analyzing 100+ projects, 500+ cohorts, 50+ organizations, and performance data from 12,000 enterprises. Introduces the SCALE framework -- five evidence-based considerations (Select, Charge, Address problems, Learn, lead by Example) that distinguish high-performing BDS programs from low-performing ones. Provides quantified impact data, cost-effectiveness benchmarks, gender/inclusion lens, and actionable design principles for BDS program improvement.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: The SCALE Framework Definition
> Five evidence-based considerations: S (Select the right enterprise), C (Charging enterprises improves performance), A (Address problems through problem-solving not lectures), L (Learn by evaluating enterprise performance), E (Lead by Example through organizational self-improvement). What distinguishes high from low performers is not practices alone but HOW they are implemented. Even small adjustments by BDS providers can lead to tangible improvements.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28A
**Relevancia:** Provides a diagnostic quality lens for the Support Services domain. Evaluating whether an ecosystem's programs incorporate SCALE considerations goes beyond counting the number of programs.

### INS-2: BDS Impact Variation Driven by Design, Not Type
> Evaluations show HIGH VARIABILITY in BDS program impacts. Even programs by the SAME provider with the SAME approach yield vastly different outcomes. Design, delivery, and evaluation drive variation MORE than service type (acceleration vs. incubation vs. consulting). High performers give entrepreneurs more time working on their own, have fewer but more qualified applicants, and emphasize networking and peer learning. Quantified: highest-performing programs generated USD 86,000 in 1-year incremental investment growth per enterprise vs. USD 28,000 for lowest.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28B
**Relevancia:** The number of accelerators/incubators is necessary but insufficient for ANDE scoring. Quality indicators -- rigorous selection, fees, problem-solving focus, impact evaluation -- matter more for ecosystem outcomes.

### INS-3: Selection -- Targeting the Right Enterprises
> 80% of BDS providers consider selection critical; almost 1/3 report difficulties. High-performing programs target enterprises with initial revenue, IP, and experienced entrepreneurs. Six dimensions: clear criteria, leveraged referrals, realistic expectations, portfolio segmentation, multi-stage process, and feedback loops. Alterna (Central America) doubled programs per year by investing in dedicated pipeline team and segmenting into "inclusion" and "impact" categories. TechnoServe achieves 80%+ referral-based selection.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28C
**Relevancia:** For nascent/emergent ecosystems, recommend that existing programs adopt rigorous selection and segmentation before scaling quantity.

### INS-4: Charging Fees Doubles Impact
> Introducing fees led to enterprises DOUBLING jobs created in HALF the time, with ROI increasing by MORE THAN 10-fold. In Jamaica, ALL microenterprises were willing to contribute at least a nominal fee. Only 65% attend fully subsidized training; paying enterprises attend more. Bpeace case: after introducing fees, a cohort generated USD 3.4M in incremental revenue and 62 jobs in ONE year vs. USD 180K and 32 jobs after TWO years previously. Applications decreased 60% but quality increased substantially.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28D
**Relevancia:** Recommend emerging ecosystems' BDS providers test nominal fees as both a sustainability mechanism AND quality-improvement intervention. Relevant to S1, S2 indicators.

### INS-5: Problem-Solving Over Traditional Training
> Traditional business training has LIMITED effect on enterprise performance. Group-based consulting (3-8 enterprises) improves management practices AS MUCH as individual consulting at ONE-THIRD the cost. A group intervention in Colombia's auto-parts sector produced 6-15 new workers, 28-33% sales growth, and 5-26% profit uptick. Peer network members exhibit median annual revenue growth higher than OECD high-growth firm definition. Village Capital caps lectures at 15% of program time, allocating the rest to implementation plans, peer feedback, and enterprise changes.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28E
**Relevancia:** For ecosystems where programs exist but quality is low, recommend problem-based approaches, peer-learning structures, and reduced lecture time. Relevant to S1, S2, S4.

### INS-6: Learning -- Evaluating Enterprise Performance
> No strong correlation between participant satisfaction and improved business performance. BDS results may take 2-3 years to become evident. Three essential metrics: revenue, full-time employees, and investment levels. Relational data collection (personal calls from known contacts) outperforms extractive processes (surveys alone). A/B testing between cohorts is recommended before committing to costly RCTs.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28F
**Relevancia:** Programs without performance measurement cannot improve. Connects to both S-domain (quality) and F-domain (demonstrating ROI to funders).

### INS-7: Organizational Development of BDS Providers
> 40-65% of accelerators take equity but exits are insufficient to cover costs for 90%; only 1 in 20 are sustained primarily through cash-for-equity. Organizations with former entrepreneurs on boards are MORE effective. Alterna diversifies funding: 30-40% from governments/international cooperation, 30-40% from corporations, 20-30% from international funders, plus client fees as unrestricted revenue.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28G
**Relevancia:** When support organizations are fragile or donor-dependent, recommend organizational development pathways alongside programmatic improvements.

### INS-8: Cost-Effectiveness Benchmarks
> Alterna: USD 6,700/enterprise, USD 3,700/job, 40% median CAGR. Bpeace: USD 16,500/enterprise, USD 5,200/job. TechnoServe: USD 6,100/enterprise, USD 2,120/job. Village Capital: USD 25,500/enterprise, USD 3,100/job, 139% median CAGR. Village Capital alumni raise 3x more capital, earn 2.3x more revenue, create 40% more jobs vs. non-participants.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28H
**Relevancia:** Use as benchmarks when recommending cost-effective BDS models. For nascent/emerging ecosystems with limited funding, lower cost-per-enterprise models (TechnoServe, Alterna) may be more appropriate.

### INS-9: SGB Segmentation -- Four Enterprise Profiles
> SGBs are not monolithic. Four profiles: High-Growth Ventures (100+ employees, experienced founders ~45 years old), Niche Ventures (innovation-driven, smaller market), Dynamic Enterprises (USD 200K-3M revenue, often family businesses), and Formalizing Enterprises (transitioning from informal to formal). Each segment requires different BDS program designs. One-size-fits-all approaches underperform.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28I
**Relevancia:** When analyzing ecosystem support services, consider whether programs are properly segmented for different enterprise types or applying one-size-fits-all approaches.

### INS-10: Gender and Inclusion Lens for BDS
> Women-owned firms have 34% lower profits on average; female-led enterprises show 82% profit gap vs. male-led. VilCap Investments' peer-selection method resulted in 46% female-led portfolio vs. 15% industry standard. Socio-emotional skills training (confidence, leadership, resilience) positively impacts women entrepreneurs' profits. Key recommendations: inclusive outreach language, dedicated women's networks, childcare services, disaggregated data by gender.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 28K
**Relevancia:** Include gender considerations in BDS recommendations, especially for ecosystems with low female entrepreneurship participation.
