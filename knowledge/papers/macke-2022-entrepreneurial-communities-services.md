## Resumen
Practice brief (8 pp.) presenting a framework for connecting entrepreneurs with business services in ecosystems of varying sizes. Introduces Sirolli's Trinity of Management model (Production, Marketing, Finance) as an organizing principle for BDS coverage, advocates demand-driven service delivery, and proposes matching-grant/cost-sharing programs as a low-cost accelerator for nascent ecosystems. Particularly relevant for small and medium-sized cities where specialized services may not be locally available.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Trinity of Management as BDS coverage framework
> Sirolli's model identifies three capabilities every venture needs: Production, Marketing, and Finance. Few entrepreneurs excel in all three, making business development services essential. An ecosystem with incubators focused only on tech/product but lacking finance or marketing support has a critical structural gap.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 20A
**Relevancia:** Provides a diagnostic lens for S1-S5 evaluation. Instead of counting programs, assess whether the three functional areas are covered.

### INS-2: Demand-driven delivery and deal flow
> The e2 framework operates on a demand-driven principle: assistance should be designed around entrepreneurs' actual needs, not the supply of available programs. Deal flow -- defined as ventures investing in their operations -- is the mechanism through which economic impact materializes.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 20B
**Relevancia:** Explains the Condition-Result pattern: an ecosystem can have actors (Condition) but without demand-driven connection mechanisms, deal flow fails to materialize and M1 outcomes remain low.

### INS-3: Matching grant / cost-sharing programs as ecosystem accelerator
> Push strategy: condition access to financing on obtaining specific technical assistance (e.g., growth planning). Pull strategy: incentivize entrepreneurs to hire professional services by subsidizing the first year. Both are point-in-time, cost-sharing interventions -- not permanent subsidies -- designed to create sustainable provider-client relationships.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 20C
**Relevancia:** High-impact, low-cost recommendation for Naciente/Emergente ecosystems where the rubric indicates "Build" in Support Services. Applicable to P3 and S1-S4 recommendations.

### INS-4: Virtuous cycle of business services
> Four-stage progression: (1) increase access to services via matching grants, reducing failure risk; (2) grow the service provider sector in capacity and offerings; (3) successful locally-rooted ventures generate committed stakeholders and CED leadership; (4) increased giveback -- community investors, mentors, volunteers, philanthropists.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 20D
**Relevancia:** Explains why Support Services (S) is a Condition domain: strengthening it triggers a cycle that eventually improves Culture (C) and Finance (F) outcomes. Prioritize S interventions first in low-scoring ecosystems.

### INS-5: Service mapping by functional area
> Detailed breakdown of services under each Trinity pillar: Production (technology, HR, supply chain, R&D), Marketing (branding, digital, e-commerce, customer research), Finance (accounting, legal, tax, risk management, POS systems). A mature ecosystem should cover all three areas; small cities may rely on regional access rather than local presence.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 20E
**Relevancia:** Operational checklist for S1-S5 assessment. When evaluating incubators and BDS providers, check which functional areas they cover rather than just counting organizations.

### INS-6: Innovation in service access for small cities and rural contexts
> Where specialized services are not locally available, ecosystems can leverage local expertise in adjacent roles (e.g., hospital HR department advising on people management) and build referral networks to regional providers. A small ecosystem with effective referral networks can outperform a larger one with formal but disconnected institutions.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 20F
**Relevancia:** Critical adjustment criterion for cities under 1M population. S1-S5 evaluation should assess connection mechanisms and regional referral capacity, not just formal institutional presence.
