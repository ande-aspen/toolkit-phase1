## Resumen
Short practitioner paper (3 pages) identifying 10 distinct movements ("E-Movements") that have shaped entrepreneur-led community economic development in the United States, presented in rough chronological order from Chambers of Commerce (1773) through Ecosystem Building (late 1990s onward). Provides a diagnostic heuristic: mature ecosystems accumulate layers from multiple movements; emerging ecosystems can assess which layers are present and which are missing. Directly relevant to ANDE Culture domain and ecosystem maturity assessment.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Ten E-Movements Framework
> Ten distinct but interrelated movements that built entrepreneurial culture in the U.S.: (1) Chambers of Commerce (1773), (2) Capital-Led, (3) SBA and SBDCs (1953/1970s), (4) Technology-Led (1959 Research Triangle Park), (5) Incubation and Accelerators (first incubator 1959), (6) Microenterprise Development (1976 Grameen Bank), (7) Economic Gardening (1987 Littleton, CO), (8) Training-Led (1993 Kauffman FastTrac), (9) SourceLink (2003 UMKC), (10) Ecosystem Building (late 1990s onward). These represent accumulating institutional layers in a mature ecosystem.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30A
**Relevancia:** When diagnosing a city, assess which of these layers exist and which are missing. Emerging Latin American economies may be building several simultaneously rather than sequentially. Naciente ecosystems should not be expected to have all 10 layers.

### INS-2: Chambers of Commerce as First-Generation Ecosystem Builders
> Likely the oldest form of organized entrepreneurial development in the U.S. (first chamber: Charleston, SC, 1773). Originally provided direct services, networking, and relationship-building. Over time, evolved into primarily promotion and advocacy, moving away from direct entrepreneurial services.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30B
**Relevancia:** In Latin America, chambers of commerce (camaras de comercio) often still play a direct services role. Assess whether local chambers are active ecosystem participants or have become purely advocacy-oriented. The transition pattern may indicate a gap that newer organizations need to fill.

### INS-3: Capital-Led Movement and Its Persistence
> Access to capital has been a long-running focus. Early examples: Kentucky Highlands, Maine's Coastal Enterprises, Michigan's Northern Initiatives. Capital continues as a central driver but is just one of 10 movements -- insufficient alone.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30C
**Relevancia:** Confirms finance is a perennial bottleneck but diagnostics should assess whether an ecosystem is over-reliant on a single capital-led approach without complementary layers (training, mentoring, incubation, coordination).

### INS-4: Incubation and Acceleration as Micro-Ecosystems
> Macke reframes incubators and accelerators as "micro-entrepreneurial ecosystems" focused on a specific cohort. First U.S. incubator: Batavia, NY, 1959.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30D
**Relevancia:** Instead of counting accelerators only as standalone support organizations (S1, S2), assess whether these programs function as coherent micro-ecosystems integrating multiple support elements (capital, mentoring, training, networking). A single well-integrated accelerator may contribute more than several fragmented programs.

### INS-5: Microenterprise Development -- Inclusion Dimension
> Movement traced to Muhammad Yunus and Grameen Bank (Bangladesh, 1976). Focus on smaller ventures, appropriate capital, and technical assistance. Represents the inclusive dimension of entrepreneurial ecosystems.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30E
**Relevancia:** ANDE diagnostics focused only on tech startups and high-growth ventures miss a large segment. For LatAm ecosystems, microenterprise programs are often the most widespread form of support. Diagnostics should distinguish between microenterprise support (survival/subsistence) and growth-oriented support (PECs/SGBs).

### INS-6: Economic Gardening -- Market Intelligence as Intervention
> Founded by Chris Gibbons in Littleton, CO (1987) in response to Martin Marietta workforce cuts. Core service: market intelligence research and analysis for existing businesses. A demand-side intervention rarely seen in Latin America.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30F
**Relevancia:** For ecosystems where Markets domain (M1-M3) scores low, Economic Gardening-style programs could be recommended: provide startups and SGBs with market research, competitive intelligence, and sector analysis to accelerate growth without requiring new capital injection.

### INS-7: SourceLink -- Ecosystem Coordination Infrastructure
> Created in 2003 by Maria Meyers at UMKC. First system: KCSourceLink. Now nationwide. Many SourceLink organizations now lead ecosystem-building efforts.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30H
**Relevancia:** Represents a "backbone organization" concept that catalogs resources, connects entrepreneurs to services, and reduces search costs. When Support Services shows many actors but low effectiveness, a SourceLink-type coordination layer may be what is missing.

### INS-8: Ecosystem Building as the Integrative Movement
> Intellectual genealogy: Brian Dabson/Jay Kayne/Tom Lyons framed "Entrepreneurial Development Systems," Ernesto Sirolli developed Enterprise Facilitation, Victor Hwang/Greg Horowitt captured the ecosystem concept in "The Rainforest." The ecosystem frame incorporates complexity theory -- complex and reinforcing social norms, perceptions, behaviors, and culture. Ecosystem building subsumes elements of all previous 9 movements.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30I
**Relevancia:** The presence of explicit ecosystem building activity (backbone organizations, coordination platforms, ecosystem strategies) is an indicator of maturity. Its absence suggests individual components exist but are not integrated into a coherent system. Culture is not ancillary -- it is the substrate on which all other domains operate.

### INS-9: Chronological Pattern -- Evolutionary Sequence
> Rough evolutionary sequence from networking and advocacy (1773), to credit access (1950s), to innovation (1959), to cohort-based support (1959), to inclusion (1976), to market intelligence (1987), to education (1993), to coordination (2003), to systemic integration (late 1990s+). Earlier layers correspond to earlier maturity; mature ecosystems accumulate all layers.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 30J
**Relevancia:** The ANDE intervention matrix (Build/Strengthen/Scale/Maintain by maturity level) aligns with this evolutionary logic. Nascent ecosystems should not be expected to have all layers; the diagnostic should assess which layers are present and whether they match the maturity level.
