## Resumen
GIZ provides a practical 73-page toolkit for mapping entrepreneurial ecosystems in developing economies. The guide defines ecosystems through three interconnected elements (business environment, interacting actors, entrepreneurial culture), proposes a 5-step mapping methodology executable in approximately 4 weeks, and offers comprehensive actor classification, benchmark scorecard, indicator frameworks, visualization methods, and a challenge-goal-intervention pathway from diagnosis to action. Directly references and is compatible with the ANDE toolkit.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Three-element ecosystem framework
> GIZ structures the ecosystem as (1) business environment and investment climate (legal, regulatory), (2) interacting actors clustered by Isenberg's 6 domains, and (3) entrepreneurial culture and attitude. Core principle: ecosystems cannot be created or designed by an outside actor -- interventions should improve conditions, not engineer outcomes.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13A
**Relevancia:** Reinforces that ecosystem diagnosis must address all three elements simultaneously. Addressing one in isolation produces perverse outcomes. Aligns with ANDE's multi-domain approach.

### INS-2: Five-step mapping methodology with timeline benchmarks
> GIZ prescribes a 4-week process: (1) Research design (1 day), (2) Data collection via desk research + interviews (2 weeks), (3) Scorecard analysis and actor visualization (5 days), (4) Stakeholder validation workshop (2 days), (5) Transition from mapping to intervention design (1 week+). Start with secondary data, identify gaps, then fill with primary data.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13B
**Relevancia:** Provides timeline benchmarks for ecosystem assessments. Culture and attitude data relies most heavily on primary sources since benchmark reports rarely cover it.

### INS-3: Comprehensive actor classification by ecosystem domain
> GIZ categorizes actors across Finance (banks, microfinance, fintech, VCs, angels, DFIs), Support (incubators, accelerators, associations, co-working spaces), Policy (national/regional/local government, regulators), Markets (domestic/international corporations, e-commerce), Human Capital (universities, training institutes), and Media (TV, social media, role models). Additional categories for green/inclusive and women entrepreneurship.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13C
**Relevancia:** Cross-reference with ANDE's 30-indicator framework to ensure all actor categories are represented. Useful for actor mapping in Section 4 of diagnostic reports.

### INS-4: Benchmark reports scorecard for tracking ecosystem evolution
> GIZ recommends tracking international rankings over time: Doing Business (World Bank), WEF Competitiveness, GEM, Global Innovation Index (WIPO), GEDI Index, Enterprise Surveys, VC/PE Country Attractiveness (IESE), BTI (Bertelsmann). Critical note: GEM is the only benchmark that covers culture/attitude with primary data.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13D
**Relevancia:** Identifies which ANDE indicators can be sourced from benchmarks versus requiring primary data collection. For countries not in GEM, culture assessment requires interviews.

### INS-5: Five visualization methods for diagnostic reporting
> GIZ offers: (1) Actor category map (bubble diagram by domain), (2) Capacity WORKS stakeholder map (concentric circles showing cooperation intensity), (3) Abstract ecosystem visualization (bubble sizes + color-coded capacity needs), (4) Regional distribution map (geographic gaps), (5) Business phase mapping (actor types vs. business phases revealing "valley of death" gaps).

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13F
**Relevancia:** Business-phase mapping is especially useful for identifying where ecosystems fail to support transitions from early stage to growth -- a common bottleneck in developing economies.

### INS-6: Challenge-goal-intervention framework
> GIZ structures the pathway from diagnostic findings to action: identify the challenge (e.g., no finance for growth-phase entrepreneurs), define the goal (improve access to growth-stage finance), and specify possible interventions (detailed gap assessment, advisory on international financing). Examples cover business environment, actors, and culture domains.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13G
**Relevancia:** Provides a template for structuring actionable recommendations in Section 7 of ANDE reports. Ensures recommendations are tied to specific diagnostic findings.

### INS-7: Hypothesis-driven archetypes for developing economies
> Common patterns include: no national entrepreneurship strategy, physical space required for business registration, no crowdfunding framework, no formal PPD mechanism, oversupply of early-stage support but undersupply for later stages, business angels increasingly risk-averse, public sector jobs considered more prestigious, fear of failure preventing business creation, very low female TEA.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13H
**Relevancia:** These archetypes serve as comparison patterns when analyzing specific cities. Particularly relevant for developing economy ecosystems where several of these hypotheses typically hold simultaneously.

### INS-8: Performance indicators for longitudinal ecosystem monitoring
> Beyond diagnostic indicators, GIZ specifies outcome tracking: enterprise birth/death rate trends, 5-year survival, employment share of enterprise births, high-growth enterprise shares by sector, turnover from e-commerce, sales of new-to-market innovations, FDI, and exports.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 13I
**Relevancia:** Complements ANDE's diagnostic framework (which measures ecosystem determinants) with outcome tracking. Useful for recommending a monitoring framework in Section 7.
