## Resumen
IDB/BID Lab report (37 pp.) adapting Isenberg's (2010) entrepreneurial ecosystem model to the long-term care sector in Latin America. While heavily sector-specific (caregiver training, copayment systems, nursing homes), the document provides transferable frameworks: a six-cluster Isenberg adaptation confirming alignment with ANDE domains, a five-step ecosystem development sequence, LatAm-specific barriers identified through focus groups in 5 countries with 36 professionals, and an actor mapping template that includes non-obvious stakeholder types. Low overall relevance for general diagnostics, but specific insights on LatAm barriers and sequencing methodology are valuable.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Six-cluster Isenberg adaptation confirms ANDE domain mapping
> The report adapts Isenberg's model into six clusters: Public Policies (regulatory framework, standardized contracts, quality supervision) = P; Entrepreneurial Financing (mechanisms, microcredits, insurance, copayment) = F; Entrepreneurial Culture (awareness campaigns, sector revaluation) = C; Support Structures (incubators, knowledge exchange, innovation centers, events) = S; Human Capital Development (training, certification, cooperative leadership) = H; Access to Markets (digital portals, public-sector outsourcing, large company collaboration) = M. Validated through focus groups in 5 countries.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 25A
**Relevancia:** Confirms that Isenberg's framework maps reasonably well to the ANDE structure. The focus-group validation methodology (5 countries, 36 professionals) is a sound template for sector-specific ecosystem diagnostics.

### INS-2: Five-step sequencing for ecosystem development
> Ordered by implementation difficulty and impact: (1) Diagnostic actions -- map demand and supply at municipality level. (2) Key opportunities / quick wins -- regulatory framework, standardized contracts, training programs, digital portals, quality certification. (3) Strategic investments -- public/collaborative financing, microcredit, incubators/accelerators, innovation hubs. (4) Support actions -- awareness campaigns, insurance, events (hackathons, fairs). (5) Consolidation actions -- large-small company collaboration, comprehensive well-being initiatives. Elements do not function in isolation but continuously interact and reinforce one another.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 25B
**Relevancia:** Parallels ANDE Table 4.3 intervention sequencing by maturity level. The emphasis on municipality-level diagnostic and the explicit statement that ecosystem elements continuously interact reinforces the Condition-Result pattern analysis approach.

### INS-3: LatAm-specific ecosystem barriers from focus groups
> From 5 focus groups (Spain, Uruguay, Costa Rica, Colombia, Brazil) with 36 sector professionals: (1) Regulatory delays blocking market entry -- one-year patent/certification waits reported in Uruguay. (2) Bureaucratic fragmentation forcing navigation of multiple agencies, with strong desire for one-stop shops. (3) Cultural undervaluation of certain sectors inhibiting entrepreneurial interest and talent attraction. (4) Financing gaps as the most-cited barrier, especially for infrastructure-intensive businesses. (5) Informal support networks (post-event conversations at accelerators) generate synergies that formal programs cannot replicate.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 25C
**Relevancia:** Broadly applicable beyond the care sector. Check for: time-to-market regulatory bottlenecks beyond incorporation time, one-stop-shop integration, cultural perception of dominant sectors, and informal networking density alongside formal programs. The informal networks finding supports evaluating C1/C4 beyond formal meetup counts.

### INS-4: Actor mapping template with non-obvious stakeholder types
> Key actor types per cluster: Public Policies (municipality, ministry, civic boards, regional government), Financing (VC, angels, public funders, grant entities, insurance companies), Culture (journalists, influencers, entertainment industry), Support Structures (integration facilitators, entrepreneur association groups), Human Capital (training schools public/private, professional cooperatives), Markets (large companies, digital platform creators).

**Ubicación:** Extracto 25D
**Relevancia:** Includes non-obvious actors often missed in standard mapping: journalists and influencers in Culture, insurance companies in Finance, professional cooperatives in Human Capital. In LatAm, cooperatives are frequently overlooked but can be significant in organizing informal-sector talent.
