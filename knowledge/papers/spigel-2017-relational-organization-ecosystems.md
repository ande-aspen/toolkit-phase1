## Resumen
Spigel identifies 10 ecosystem attributes grouped into three categories (cultural, social, material) and demonstrates through comparative case studies of Waterloo and Calgary that no single optimal ecosystem configuration exists. The key theoretical contribution is that attributes do not exist in isolation but develop in tandem, influencing and reproducing each other through relational chains. Cultural beliefs normalize support for entrepreneurship, which creates context for social attributes (networks, mentors, investment capital), which in turn enable material attributes (programs, policies) to function. Material programs imported without underlying social and cultural support are likely to fail.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Taxonomy of 10 ecosystem attributes across three categories
> Cultural attributes (2): supportive culture and entrepreneurship histories/role models. Social attributes (4): talent willing to work in startups, investment capital channeled through social networks, networks connecting entrepreneurs/advisors/investors, and mentors/role models who build social capital. Material attributes (4): policy and governance, universities, support services (accountants, lawyers, incubators, accelerators), and open markets with local customers.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 17A
**Relevancia:** Maps directly to ANDE's 7 domains: Culture=C, Talent=H, Capital=F, Networks/Mentors=S+C, Policy=P, Universities=H+I, Services=S, Markets=M. Not all attributes are necessary for a successful ecosystem -- this is a key departure from frameworks that imply all domains must be strong.

### INS-2: Relational configuration -- connections between attributes reproduce the ecosystem
> Attributes develop in tandem, influencing and reproducing each other. Cultural beliefs normalize entrepreneurship support, creating context for social attributes to emerge. Policies and programs struggle in the absence of an underlying community of entrepreneurs and advisors. The relationship is bidirectional: material and social attributes strengthen culture by creating new success stories. Ecosystems with dense inter-attribute relationships are self-reproducing; in more dispersed ecosystems, a single attribute (e.g., a large local market) drives the rest.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 17B
**Relevancia:** Fundamental for ANDE's Condition-Result pattern. An ecosystem may score well on Condition domains but fail on Result domains if connections between attributes are weak. Analysis must go beyond counting actors to assessing how domains interact.

### INS-3: Calgary -- market-driven but culturally fragile ecosystem
> Calgary is driven by its oil and gas industry: 68% of tech entrepreneurs served the energy sector. Characterized by a wealth-oriented culture (42% of entrepreneurs categorized as "profit-oriented" vs. 4% in Waterloo), abundant but sector-biased capital (investors from energy sector don't understand software/biotech risk), talent scarcity for startups (can't compete with oil company salaries), and weak support programs that lack cultural/social foundation. Networking events end up being "a bunch of people just like themselves."

**Ubicación:** Extracto 17C
**Relevancia:** Warning case for ecosystems with high Markets score but low Culture and Support. A high Markets score can mask structural weaknesses. Reference when an ecosystem shows "fragile system" pattern (low Condition, relatively high Result driven by one sector).

### INS-4: Waterloo -- dense ecosystem driven by culture and networks
> Waterloo has a deeply embedded entrepreneurial ethos: entrepreneurs feel responsibility to help others, CEOs of large companies respond when asked to mentor startups. Dense and functional networks where sharing experiences is an essential part of being an entrepreneur there. No talent retention problems (normalized working in startups). Communitech serves as anchor organization, but would not have functioned without pre-existing cultural and social support from local leaders. University of Waterloo has favorable IP regime incentivizing spinoffs.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 17D
**Relevancia:** Model where Culture and Support are the primary drivers. Demonstrates that support organizations (S) need a cultural base (C) to function. Use as positive reference when an ecosystem shows strong conditions but needs to strengthen culture-to-program connections.

### INS-5: Multiple configurations -- no single model for ecosystem success
> Calgary produces larger, higher-revenue firms (avg. 25.5 employees, $3.7M revenue) through its energy market, but with less innovation and lower social connectivity. Waterloo produces VC-oriented startups focused on IPO/acquisition exits (avg. 10.3 employees, $1.1M revenue) with high peer-seeking behavior (70% seek peer advice vs. 46% in Calgary). Central conclusion: there is no single optimal configuration. What matters is how attributes reinforce each other. Importing best practices without considering local cultural and social attributes is doomed to fail.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 17E
**Relevancia:** For Section 7 recommendations: interventions must adapt to local configuration. Do not recommend programs (material) without evaluating whether cultural and social foundations exist. ANDE's maturity-based intervention table (Table 4.3) should be complemented with this logic: build culture and networks BEFORE creating formal programs.

### INS-6: Policy implication -- build social and cultural base before programs
> New material attributes (support programs, investment schemes, technology transfer programs) have low probability of success if not backed by complementary social and cultural attributes. Regional entrepreneurship policy should focus on building underlying support for new programs, not expect that the programs themselves will create cultures and networks.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 17F
**Relevancia:** Directly aligned with ANDE's evolutionary sequence (Culture -> Human Capital -> Support -> Policy -> Finance -> Markets -> R&D). A nascent ecosystem must build Culture first, not programs. Use as theoretical backing in Section 7 when recommending culture-building over infrastructure of support.
