## Resumen
Practice brief (9 pp.) distinguishing growth-oriented entrepreneurs (GOEs) from lifestyle business owners and providing a framework for community strategies to identify and support them. Introduces the Edward Lowe Foundation stage classification, establishes that high-growth firms represent less than 5% of all businesses yet generate up to two-thirds of job creation, and maps GOE needs across all ANDE domains. Includes case studies of market intelligence programs (Economic Gardening) showing dramatic sales growth. Though US-focused, the segmentation framework and support strategies are directly applicable to ecosystem diagnostics globally.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Growth-oriented entrepreneur definition and economic impact
> GOEs have both a strong desire for growth AND the potential capacity to realize sustained growth, seeking external markets and bringing new wealth into the local economy. Key distinction from lifestyle businesses: GOEs think about scaling from day one. High-growth companies represent less than 5% of all businesses yet create up to two-thirds of all business growth and job creation, and generate up to 50% of all innovation. High-growth firms exist in most regions and across ALL industries, not just high-tech.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 23A (pp. 2-3)
**Relevancia:** Critical for interpreting M1 (Startups activas). Raw startup count matters less than the proportion of growth-oriented ventures. The less-than-5% statistic contextualizes why ecosystem interventions should be targeted rather than broad-based.

### INS-2: Business stage framework and age vs. size
> Edward Lowe Foundation classification: Self-employed (1 employee), Stage 1 (2-9), Stage 2 (10-99, revenue ~$750K-$50M), Stage 3 (100-499), Stage 4 (500+). Optimal target for community programs: late Stage 1 and early Stage 2 companies, 1-5 years old, growth-oriented regardless of size. Critical finding: firm age is a more significant growth driver than firm size -- younger companies create more jobs regardless of size.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 23B (pp. 3-4)
**Relevancia:** Provides a lens for M1 evaluation beyond simple counting. The age-over-size finding challenges traditional measurement approaches. Screening questions (niche competitiveness, growth commitment, external market exploration, coachability) can inform qualitative M1 assessment.

### INS-3: GOE needs hierarchy mapped to ANDE domains
> Eight needs: capital access (F1-F6, H4), strategic coaching and technical assistance (S1, S2, S4), facilities and infrastructure (S3, S5), friendly tax/regulatory environment (P1, P2), market intelligence and external market access (M3), trained workforce (H2, H4), strategic alliances (C1, C4), and management capacity (H1). Capital access challenge is more nuanced than simple shortage -- risk aversion creates tight supply even where cash is available.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 23C (pp. 4-5)
**Relevancia:** Comprehensive mapping of GOE needs to all 7 ANDE domains. Use this framework when designing Section 7 recommendations to ensure they address the full spectrum. Market intelligence as a service is particularly relevant for ecosystems with weak M3 scores.

### INS-4: Quality of life factors for entrepreneur retention
> Entrepreneurs with external market footprint can locate anywhere, making quality-of-life a competitive factor: safe communities, good schools, quality housing, cultural amenities, healthcare access. In smaller communities, community development and economic development are inseparable -- both are needed to create hope, increase wealth, and expand choices.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 23D (pp. 5-6)
**Relevancia:** While not directly measured in ANDE's 30 indicators, quality of life influences H4 (talent retention) and ecosystem attractiveness. Supports holistic strategies rather than single-domain interventions.

### INS-5: Market intelligence impact -- Economic Gardening case studies
> Solar Roast Coffee (Pueblo, CO): county GIS-based Economic Gardening program provided customer base analysis, competitor mapping, expansion area identification, demographic analysis, and funder connections. Result: multiple retail expansions, 50+ wholesale locations, university partnerships. Keyscaper (Tualatin, OR): strategic market intelligence grew sales from thousands/month to $100K+/month within one year; acquired Best Buy, Google, Boeing as customers; added 16 positions (50% staff increase).

**Ubicación:** Extracto 23E (pp. 6-7)
**Relevancia:** Market intelligence programs are lower cost than full incubation/acceleration but can produce dramatic results for existing growth-oriented businesses. The Economic Gardening model (GIS-based market analysis for local businesses) is a high-impact, quick-win recommendation for ecosystems scoring low on S1/S2.

### INS-6: Program design principles for GOE support
> Six principles: (1) start with identification and targeting -- not all businesses need the same support; (2) focus specifically on growth-oriented entrepreneurs, not all SMEs; (3) market intelligence is a high-leverage, scalable intervention; (4) combine community development with economic development; (5) use data and analytics as foundation; (6) build networks and coalitions across institutions. Ecosystem-building organizations themselves are critical infrastructure.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 23F (pp. 8-9)
**Relevancia:** Aligns with ANDE Table 4.3 sequencing -- focus support on the right target population before scaling. The principle that ecosystem-building organizations are critical infrastructure connects to the concept of backbone organizations.
