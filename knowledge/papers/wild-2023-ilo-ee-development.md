## Resumen
Comprehensive ILO study (114 pages) mapping the global landscape of entrepreneurial ecosystem development (EED), based on 18 interviews with EE leaders, 30 academic articles, and 100+ secondary materials from 31 EED organizations. Proposes a four-category actor taxonomy, critiques static frameworks in favor of a process-oriented approach, identifies seven common shortcomings in diagnostics and six in measurement, and offers recommendations for implementers, funders, and thinkers. Directly relevant to ANDE diagnostic methodology as a critical self-assessment lens.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: Four Actor Categories for Ecosystem Builders
> Four categories of EED actors: (1) Thinkers -- universities, think tanks that investigate and publish research; (2) Informers/Advisors -- provide data or context-specific guidance (GEI, GEM, Startup Genome, consulting firms); (3) Funders -- provide funding for EED itself (foundations, bilateral/multilateral donors, governments); (4) Implementers -- take action in the ecosystem (global NGOs, governments, ESOs, entrepreneur groups). Implementers further subdivide by inside/outside the ecosystem, short-term/long-term, and whole-of-ecosystem/limited scope.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29A
**Relevancia:** Use this taxonomy when mapping actors. An ecosystem with many implementers but no thinkers or informers may be action-heavy but knowledge-poor. An ecosystem where all actors are outside/short-term signals fragility.

### INS-2: Five Categories of Critical Success Factors (31 Specific Factors)
> Analysis of 27 EE frameworks reveals five categories: Actors, Functions/Roles, Policy, Conditions, and Relational Characteristics. Most common factors (~70% of frameworks): finance/capital and human capital/talent. Most frameworks focus on static factors and underrepresent relational characteristics (network density, power/leadership, collaboration, trust, diversity, inclusiveness). These semi-implicit and implicit factors are harder to identify but critical for system functioning.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29B
**Relevancia:** The ANDE 7-domain model covers actors, functions, policy, and conditions well. The gap is in relational characteristics. When writing Section 5 analysis, explicitly assess trust, collaboration, network density, inclusiveness, and power dynamics -- not captured by the 30 indicators but critical for interpreting scores.

### INS-3: The "Don't-Copy-Copy" Paradox and Process-Oriented Approach
> Fundamental paradox: system builders say each ecosystem is unique (don't copy), yet develop frameworks implying replication of critical success factors will produce success (do copy). The study argues for a shift from factor-oriented to process-oriented approach. Key question changes from "what factors need to exist?" to "what process should we follow to catalyze change?" Six-step process: define boundaries, select ecosystem, understand it, design evidence-based interventions, implement adaptively, and monitor system outcomes.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29C
**Relevancia:** The ANDE Phase 1 diagnostic is essentially step 3 of this process. Validates the approach but warns against stopping at actor/function mapping. Section 7 recommendations should be framed as process steps (what to do, how to learn, how to adapt) rather than just prescribing factors to build.

### INS-4: Seven Common Shortcomings of EED Diagnostics
> (1) Mapping as inventory, not analysis -- inventorying actors without understanding interactions; (2) Too macro -- identifying challenges without underlying causes; (3) Missing relational characteristics -- no assessment of network density, connectivity, power dynamics; (4) No focus on triggers of change; (5) No leverage point analysis; (6) Combined baseline and design -- trying to do both at once and doing neither well; (7) Not target-group specific -- diagnostics based on general frameworks rather than specific entrepreneur segments. Swisscontact observes that ANDE Snapshot Toolkit and GIZ mapping are powerful for mapping ESOs but do not consider interactions and dynamics.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29D
**Relevancia:** Applies directly to ANDE Phase 1. To strengthen the diagnostic: add qualitative notes on network density and collaboration quality; identify 2-3 leverage points (most influential actors); assess underlying causes, not just symptoms (e.g., if financing is low, is it because no funds exist or because funds and entrepreneurs cannot find each other?).

### INS-5: Six Common Shortcomings of EED Measurement
> (1) No system change measurement -- most measure outputs (entrepreneurs trained) not system change (shifts in actor interactions); (2) No contribution analysis; (3) Missing theories of change; (4) No unintended consequences measurement; (5) Benchmark paradox -- using comparable regions as benchmarks contradicts the principle that every system is different; (6) Anecdotal case studies with confirmation bias. SECO funds Swiss EP over 12 years as a positive exception to short funding cycles.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29E
**Relevancia:** Section 7 should include recommendations for establishing the ecosystem's own measurement baseline and monitoring system. Track change from the ecosystem's own starting point rather than primarily comparing to other ecosystems. Also recommend tracking relational indicators alongside output indicators.

### INS-6: Ecosystem Lifecycle -- Synthesis and Critique
> Compiles models from Startup Genome, ANDE, Endeavor, Mack Mayer, and Cantner et al. General consensus: EEs develop organically and non-linearly, are self-organizing and self-sustaining. Key critique: lifecycle models are mostly based on tech startups in high-income countries, framed too much from an investor perspective (exits, early-stage funding, unicorns), and do not translate well to developing economies. Insufficient discussion on what factors predict or help move an ecosystem to the next phase.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29F
**Relevancia:** The Endeavor framework (externally dependent, emerging local, self-propelling) adds a useful dimension: does the ecosystem depend on external actors or is it locally driven? When scoring maturity, note the degree of external dependency as a qualitative factor.

### INS-7: Three "Systemsiness" Groupings -- Self-Assessment Framework
> Group 1 ("working in the ecosystem"): uses EED label but does traditional entrepreneurship development, focuses on actor capacity not underlying causes, no systemic change measurement. Group 2 ("working on the ecosystem"): follows systems change principles but cannot prove change, understands relational factors, adapts approach based on learning. Group 3 (full systems change): understands explicit/semi-explicit/implicit factors, addresses underlying causes, measures system change and contribution, monitors unintended consequences.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29G
**Relevancia:** Assess whether local organizations (accelerators, government agencies, foundations) operate at Group 1, 2, or 3. This assessment strengthens the Support Services domain analysis and Section 7 recommendations.

### INS-8: Recommendations for Funders
> Funders collectively provide too much funding in some ecosystems, crowding out private actors. Individual EED efforts are often underfunded. Funding cycles are generally too short (2-3 years) vs. system change needs (years/decades). Funders do not require systemic change measurement. Too much focus on employment creation outcomes, not enough on broader value creation or inclusion.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29H
**Relevancia:** When assessing the Finance domain, consider development funding flowing into the ecosystem alongside private investment. Is there funder crowding? Are programs too short-term? Is development funding creating dependency rather than self-sustaining capacity?

### INS-9: Urban Focus and Developing Economy Challenges
> EED is predominantly urban-focused and concentrated in high-income countries. Within countries, it targets capital cities and commercial hubs. EEs naturally evolve in urban environments due to higher concentration of actors and density-dependent vibrant communities. Most EED focuses on tech entrepreneurship and formally registered startups/young firms.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 29I
**Relevancia:** When diagnosing smaller or non-capital LatAm cities, do not assume benchmark data from Mexico City or Bogota applies. Smaller cities will naturally have fewer actors and less density; the strategy should focus on connectivity to larger hubs rather than replicating capital-city infrastructure locally.
