## Resumen
Isenberg's foundational article defines the entrepreneurship ecosystem as six interconnected domains (Policy, Finance, Culture, Support, Human Capital, Markets) and distills nine prescriptions for ecosystem building drawn from global case studies (Israel, Taiwan, Rwanda, Ireland, France). This is the direct theoretical basis for the ANDE Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Diagnostic Toolkit. The core argument: most governmental efforts fail because they address only one or two elements, and no ecosystem should try to emulate Silicon Valley.

## Insights Clave

### INS-1: The six-domain framework
> Isenberg defines six ecosystem domains: Policy (leadership + government institutions), Finance (micro-loans through public capital markets), Culture (societal norms around success, risk, failure, status), Support (nonprofits, professions, infrastructure), Human Capital (labour + education), and Markets (early customers + networks). In isolation, each element is conducive to entrepreneurship but insufficient; together, they turbocharge venture creation and growth.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 14A
**Relevancia:** This is the foundational framework that ANDE adapts into 7 domains (splitting I+D from Human Capital). Every ANDE diagnostic references this framework as its theoretical basis.

### INS-2: Nine prescriptions for ecosystem building
> Key prescriptions: (1) Stop emulating Silicon Valley -- it evolved under unreplicable circumstances. (2) Shape ecosystem around local conditions. (3) Engage private sector first. (4) Favor high-potential entrepreneurs -- one 500-person rapidly globalizing operation creates more wealth, inspiration, and reputational value than 500 microfinanced sole proprietorships. (5) Get a big win on the board -- even one success has a surprisingly stimulating effect ("law of small numbers"). (6) Tackle cultural change directly. (7) Stress the roots -- don't flood ventures with easy money. (8) Don't overengineer clusters. (9) Reform legal and regulatory frameworks -- removing barriers beats creating incentives.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 14B
**Relevancia:** Each prescription provides a strategic lens for framing ecosystem interventions. Particularly relevant for ANDE Section 7 recommendations: local context (2), focus on growth-oriented entrepreneurs (4), success stories for culture (5), avoid easy money (7), regulatory reform (9).

### INS-3: BEEP diagnostic questions by ecosystem element
> Isenberg provides diagnostic questions via the Babson Entrepreneurship Ecosystem Project: Do public leaders advocate for entrepreneurs? Does culture tolerate failure and respect entrepreneurship? Are there experienced people who can build organizations? Are there equity sources at pre-sales stage? Do nonprofits help investors and entrepreneurs network? Are there local customers willing to try new products from new companies?

**Ubicación:** Extracto 14C
**Relevancia:** These questions can be mapped directly to ANDE's 30 indicators as qualitative validation probes. Useful for structuring stakeholder interviews in Phase 1.

### INS-4: Israel's Yozma Fund -- self-liquidating VC intervention
> In 1993, Israel created a $100M fund of funds that spawned 10 VC funds in 3 years. Each fund had Israeli government, Israeli private, and foreign private partners investing roughly equal amounts. Critical design: private partners could buy out government interest at attractive terms. Eight of 10 funds exercised this option. Government exit proved real value. The VC industry became self-sustaining. Key lesson: design in self-liquidation from the start.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 14D
**Relevancia:** Model for finance-domain interventions in developing ecosystems. Demonstrates that public capital should catalyze private markets, not substitute for them. Directly applicable when recommending VC ecosystem development.

### INS-5: Strategic principles for ecosystem intervention
> (1) Holistic approach is mandatory -- Taiwan strengthened multiple elements simultaneously. (2) Incubators are not a quick fix -- well-conceived ones can take 20+ years to generate measurable impact. (3) Removing barriers beats creating incentives. (4) The horizon always recedes -- even Massachusetts and Israel are never satisfied. (5) Private sector must share responsibility for ecosystem development.

**Ubicación:** Extracto 14E
**Relevancia:** Frames the overall strategic posture: avoid silver-bullet thinking, emphasize systemic intervention, set realistic timelines. Incubators taking 20+ years is a critical expectation-setting data point.
