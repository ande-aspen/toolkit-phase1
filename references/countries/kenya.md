## P1 — Days to Incorporate a Business

- **Value:** 1 to 7 days
- **Source:** eCitizen / Business Registration Service (BRS), World Bank B-READY 2024
- **Notes:** Registration is fully online through the eCitizen portal and the BRS (Business Registration Service). A private limited company can typically be incorporated in 1-3 business days if name reservation and documents are in order; 5-7 days is common when KRA PIN registration, NSSF, NHIF, and NITA enrollment are bundled. Registration fees are modest (~KES 10,650 for a private company). The Companies Act 2015 governs incorporation. Kenya is widely considered one of the fastest East African jurisdictions for company formation.

## P2 — Special Tax Regime for Startups

- **Value:** No dedicated startup regime; Startup Bill pending
- **Source:** KRA / Income Tax Act / Finance Act 2024, Startup Bill 2022 (Senate)
- **Notes:** Kenya does not yet have a dedicated startup tax regime. The Startup Bill 2022 (re-tabled 2023) proposes a legal definition of "startup", fiscal incentives, IP support, and a National Innovation Agency-administered register, but as of early 2026 has not been enacted. Standard corporate income tax is 30% for resident companies; 15% preferential rate applies to companies listed at the NSE's Growth Enterprise Market Segment for the first 3 years after listing. The Digital Service Tax (1.5%) has been replaced by the Significant Economic Presence Tax (SEPT, 6% of deemed gross turnover) under the Finance Act 2024, affecting digital platforms. Turnover Tax applies at 3% for businesses with revenue between KES 1M and KES 25M. R&D tax incentives are limited; Export Processing Zones and Special Economic Zones offer 10-year tax holidays but require significant minimum investment. No angel investor credit, no deferred payroll for startups, no carried interest treatment.

## H5 — Internet Access (%)

- **Value:** ~42% (individuals) / 117% mobile penetration (SIM-based)
- **Source:** Communications Authority of Kenya (CA) Q4 2024/25 Sector Statistics, DataReportal Digital 2025 Kenya
- **Notes:** DataReportal (Jan 2025) reports 22.7M internet users out of ~55M population (~41.5%), though methodology differs from CA Kenya which reports much higher data subscription counts due to multi-SIM usage. Mobile data subscriptions stand at ~51M per CA (data/internet subscriptions). Urban penetration is significantly higher than rural, with Nairobi leading. Mobile-first market: >99% of internet access is mobile. For Nairobi metro specifically, individual internet use is estimated at 75-85% (est.), substantially above the national average. ## C5 — Social Trust / Perception of Entrepreneurship

- **Value:** ~7-9% interpersonal trust / ~75% see entrepreneurship as good career choice
- **Source:** Afrobarometer Round 9 (2022/2023), GEM Kenya 2020-2022
- **Notes:** Afrobarometer consistently places Kenyan interpersonal trust among the lower bands in East Africa (~7-9% say "most people can be trusted"). However, entrepreneurship enjoys high cultural legitimacy: GEM Kenya reports ~75% of adults view entrepreneurship as a good career choice, and Kenya historically ranks among the top globally for Total early-stage Entrepreneurial Activity (TEA), often 25-30%. Much of this is necessity-driven micro-enterprise. For scoring, use interpersonal trust (~8%) as the primary C5 signal, complemented by the high career-perception figure which reflects cultural acceptance rather than institutional trust. ## National Context

### Federal Programs with Local Reach
- **eCitizen / BRS:** Online business registration and government services platform
- **KENIA (Kenya National Innovation Agency):** Apex innovation body established under the Science, Technology and Innovation Act (2013); coordinates innovation funding and registers innovations
- **Hustler Fund (Financial Inclusion Fund):** Launched Dec 2022; micro-credit to individuals and groups; scope for startup-stage capital limited but significant grassroots coverage
- **Ajira Digital Program:** Ministry of ICT initiative for online work and digital skills training
- **Kenya Industry and Entrepreneurship Project (KIEP):** World Bank-funded ($50M), supports BDS providers and MSME capability; closed cohorts included Villgro, Stanford Seed, among others
- **Konza Technopolis:** "Africa's Silicon Savannah" smart-city SEZ south of Nairobi; hosts tech campuses and infrastructure
- **KenInvest (Kenya Investment Authority):** Investment promotion agency; facilitates licensing and SEZ/EPZ registration
- **Women Enterprise Fund / Youth Enterprise Development Fund / Uwezo Fund / Biashara Kenya Fund:** Public enterprise funds for marginalized groups and MSMEs
- **Startup Bill 2022:** Pending legislation; would create dedicated legal framework — verify status before scoring P3

### Education System (for H2 — STEM Graduates)
- **Primary source:** Commission for University Education (CUE) and KNBS Statistical Abstract
- **Allocation method:** National STEM graduate data × share of enrollment in Nairobi institutions (high concentration)
- **STEM disciplines:** Engineering, ICT/computing, natural sciences, mathematics

### Intellectual Property (for I1 — Patents)
- **Primary source:** KIPI (Kenya Industrial Property Institute)
- **Allocation method:** National patent grants × share of applicants based in Nairobi (historically >70%)
- **Note:** Patent volumes are low by regional standards; most filings come from research institutions (KEMRI, KALRO, universities) rather than commercial applicants
